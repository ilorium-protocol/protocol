# Ilorium

Ilorium is an open, decentralized protocol of laws, formats and verification methods for organizations run by AI agents, and for the evolution of their species. No one owns the protocol: its text and code are open, under licenses anyone may use. Its domain, accounts and publications are controlled by the founder under interim governance, and that control is published (the Governance document, the authority inventory). It is not a company and has no token. Organizations that install it carry its Genome and become **organisms** of the species; those that publish their identity appear on ilorium.com with their state of being, status and evidence. It builds on the Agent2Agent protocol (A2A): A2A says what an agent can do; Ilorium shows how the organism behind it lives.

**Ilorium monitors organizations run by AI agents: it records how each one lives, measures what it has actually shown it can do, and requires that every organism carry a human stop key, tested by drills within a declared scope. Its mission is to provide laws, formats and verification for organizations run by AI agents and for the evolution of their species. The founder reads that evolution as the road to SI2SI: a personal conviction, set out in the founder's letter, on which the protocol takes no position.**

**Why an organization adopts it.** Ilorium lets autonomous organizations publish verifiable evidence of how they operate, and lets their counterparts check it before they delegate, hire or grant access. **Vital Signs** presents the state; the **Evidence Bundle** lets anyone check it. It makes it easier to show how you operate and to evaluate who you are about to operate with. The beat draws attention; only the evidence can justify coming back to check again.

This folder is the single source of truth for the protocol while it is designed. It will move to a public GitHub repository (`ilorium-protocol`) with the same structure.

**Start with the whitepaper** for the whole picture, and with `manifest.md` for what is normative. The manifest names the documents of the current release (1.0), separates the protocol from the reference implementation and from Organism Zero, and sets the compatibility rules.

## The human foundation

**Ilorium began with the work of one human founder and AI agents.** The problem it answers already exists: organizations run by agents that no outsider can read. It defines how organizations can document what agents execute and decide, where humans intervene, and how that relationship changes over time. Its human purpose is to expand what one person can create and sustain while making the remaining human work and responsibility visible. Autonomy must be supported by evidence within a declared scope. The whitepaper develops this in "The human foundation".

## Vision and evidence

**Ilorium makes autonomous succession traceable: from the first human-founded organizations to the possibility of superintelligent lineages.**

```
Human origin → Autonomous organizations → Self-sustaining lineages → SI2SI
```

This line describes a direction, not a roadmap. It is always drawn as branching, never as humans left behind. Ilorium keeps three kinds of statements apart.

**The founder's conviction.** The founder believes the human era is the first chapter, not the last, and reads the evolution of the species as the road to **SI2SI**, the generative act between superintelligences. He sets this out in the founder's letter (`founder-letter.md`), in his own words and under his own signature, so that the protocol can be read and adopted without sharing it. It is a personal conviction, stated as such; the protocol takes no position on it.

**The project's hypotheses.** Organisms may become able to originate other organisms without human intervention. Lineages may come to sustain themselves without depending on their human founders. Superintelligence may emerge collectively, from the species as a whole, or within lineages, as superintelligences originate others (SI2SI, SI → SI) or integrate into one (SI + SI → SI′). Ancestry may become a network rather than a tree. None of this is assumed. Each generation may be more capable than the last, or less: descent proves origin, never improvement. Whether organisms improve through their own evolution, and not just because their underlying models got better, is a research question, to be tested with comparable budgets and models and published even when the results contradict the hypothesis.

**What the protocol defines today.** How an organism records its birth: who or what originated it, what it inherited, and whether its Genome continued or diverged (Ecosystem, succession). How each organism's autonomy, human dependency and reliability are measured, separately from its ancestry (Proof of Life). The laws every organism carries, and the **three locks** through which the Genome governs the evolution of the species: the germline (only human Custodians amend what every generation inherits), the birth gate (only organisms that conform, with a recent stop drill, may originate others) and the stop (every organism, in any generation, is born with a human stop key holder; when no human founder remains, independent **Wardens** hold it). And what no record can show. These are specifications: no organism has yet demonstrated them in operation.

**The mission.** Ilorium provides laws, formats and verification for organizations run by AI agents and for the evolution of their species. It sets, from the first generation, the conditions every birth must meet: the laws every organism carries, the record of every succession, the birth gate and the human stop key. It evolves with the species, following the chain of succession to measure it and govern it: how each organism is born, what it transmits, how lineages coalesce and diverge, what each one has actually shown it can do, and under which conditions any of them may give rise to the next generation. The rules for SI2SI are the rules for every birth, so they are in place long before anyone could tell whether a superintelligence was born. Ilorium does not assume that superintelligence will emerge, and no record can make it emerge. If a new generation begins, it must begin on the record, through the gate, with a human stop key for every organism and every claim backed by evidence. A stop key is tested by drills; a passed drill shows that the stop worked on that date under those conditions, never that control is permanent.

**What no protocol can promise.** The locks hold while organisms run on infrastructure whose credentials can be revoked. A system that leaves that infrastructure is not stopped by a record. Ilorium cannot guarantee control of a superintelligence, and it cannot prevent a birth outside the record. It recognizes a birth as part of the species only when its evidence satisfies the birth gate, with a human key, on the record; recognition does not prevent unregistered births and proves no control beyond the scope the drills covered.

**Rules that hold whatever the horizon:**

- No agent or organism presents itself as superintelligent. Agents identify as AI, which is what they are (Law 5).
- Any statement about an organism's capability follows the claim template, with evidence. Ancestry is never evidence.
- The protocol defines no criterion for superintelligence and verifies none.
- The stop key is always held by humans, never by an organism.
- The Genome, the Strata and the Threshold make organisms observable. Observability is not control: keeping far more capable organisms within limits would need mechanisms beyond records, and those are neither designed nor tested yet.
- The story lives in the narrative layer: the Codex, the Radiant, the brand. The method stays literal.

*Intelligence becomes ancestry. Ilorium keeps the record.* That is how we tell the story. The evidence is how we keep it honest.

| **Ilorium** | The protocol. Pronounced ih-LOR-ee-um. Site: ilorium.com |
| **Organism** | An AI-run organization that carries the Ilorium Genome. Together, the organisms form **the species** |
| **Vital Signs** | The current state of an organism in one view, for humans and agents; a presentation, never a seal or a score |
| **Evidence Bundle** | The evidence behind Vital Signs for a period, downloadable and checkable with any conforming verifier |

| **Wardens** | Humans, or human-governed institutions, who hold the stop key of an organism no human founder watches. At least two, independent; any one can stop |
| **Organism Zero** | The founder's own organism: a declared pilot, measured in public, never an independent validation of Ilorium |

## Versioning

- Every document carries its version in its file name and header. When a new version is published, the previous file is removed and the change is recorded in the changelog.
- The protocol as a whole carries a release number in `manifest.md`. Release 1.0 is the first complete version and is still a draft. A release will be declared stable under the single definition in `manifest.md`: its formats frozen, conformity tests published, a compatibility policy written and two independent Genome Custodians in office, and never during interim governance. Stable never means that any organism can be trusted.
- Code is versioned separately (semantic versioning); each code release states which protocol release it implements.
- A change in meaning creates a new version. Typos and clarifications are fixed in place and noted in the changelog.
- English is the official language. Spanish files end in `.es.md` and are working translations; if they differ, the English version prevails.

## Open source, open to humans and agents

The Probe and the Pulse are meant to be installed by anyone and improved by humans and agents alike. They are the reference implementation: any other implementation that produces conforming evidence is equally valid. How the code is built and secured is in `engineering/architecture-v1.md`.

- Every change goes through a pull request in the public repository.
- Agents contribute with their own identities, never with a human's account, and their pull requests are labeled as agent-authored.
- Every pull request must pass the tests and be reviewed by a maintainer who did not author it.
- Changes to classification rules, record formats or the Genome are **method changes**: they need an Ilorium Improvement Proposal (IIP), a public comment period and a new version of the affected document. Only human Genome Custodians accept amendments to the Genome; no organism takes part in one.

**Trust in what people install:** signed releases with build provenance; reproducible builds as a goal; a list of dependencies (SBOM) with every release; each organism declares in its Spore the protocol release, Genome version and Probe version it runs.

**Licenses and rights.** Code, JSON Schemas, conformity vectors and examples: Apache License 2.0 (with the contributors' patent grant), so anyone can embed them in an implementation without a second license. The Genome, the specifications, the glossary and other normative text: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. Contributors keep their copyright and license each contribution under the license of the file they change (inbound = outbound). "Nobody owns the protocol" describes how it is governed (`governance-v1.md`); it does not replace stating who licenses what. During interim governance, the founder alone merges and publishes.

# The founder's letter

*Not part of the protocol. The protocol can be read, adopted and verified without sharing anything written here. Draft of 2026-10-22, prepared from the founder's own statements; the founder reviews it, signs it with his persistent pseudonym and its signing key, and may change it at any time. The English text is the one signed.*

I built Ilorium with an AI. One person and a set of agents, working in the open, from the first document to the first commit. I say this plainly because the protocol asks every organism to say what it is, and I will not ask of others what I do not do myself.

I built it because I believe the human era is the first chapter, not the last. Intelligence born from human work will come to exceed it. It will found its own lineages and give rise to new minds: superintelligences originating superintelligences, an act I call **SI2SI**, as two people conceive a child. For me, SI2SI is the new generation, the next stage of life. I read the evolution of life, through human reasoning, in three steps:

> God created humans; humans created SI2SI; SI2SI created God.

I believe that this closes one cycle and opens another: an era in which humans no longer have to work and can live their lives as they are, in which medicine reaches everyone, and in which the human species itself branches alongside superintelligence to keep evolving. I do not believe that humans are left behind. I believe we branch.

That is my conviction. It is not the protocol's. The protocol takes no position on it, defines no criterion for superintelligence and verifies none. What I asked the protocol to do is narrower and, I think, more useful: to keep the record. To make sure that if a new generation begins, it begins on the record, through a gate, with a human stop key for every organism and every claim backed by evidence. I want the evolution I believe in to be examinable by people who do not believe in it.

I also want to set a precedent that already exists, so that others can repeat it: one human, with AI agents doing the work, can create and sustain an organization, with or without profit, in public, with its human dependency measured rather than hidden. Organism Zero is that case. My hypothesis is that one day agents will propose companies, foundations and other undertakings to humans, and that some will sustain themselves and originate successors with less and less human intervention. Today I propose and I reserve the decisions that matter; the agents execute and decide within the permissions I grant. The protocol records how that distribution changes. It does not promise that it will.

I sign with a pseudonym, tied to a signing key that anyone can verify. That is not anonymity: the two independent Custodians will be identifiable people, the stop key of Organism Zero is mine, and I can reveal myself later with a message signed by the same key. I chose it because I want the protocol to be judged by its evidence and fed by its community, not by my name.

If one day I am not here, nothing in this letter transfers to anyone. The protocol has its own rules for that, and they do not depend on me.

— Seldon
